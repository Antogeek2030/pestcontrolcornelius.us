Ants are among the most frequent callers we hear about in the warmer months. Once a few scouts find a food source in your kitchen, a steady trail can appear almost overnight. While professional treatment is sometimes necessary for established colonies, a number of everyday habits can make your kitchen far less attractive to ants in the first place.

## Clean Up Crumbs and Spills Quickly

Ants are opportunistic. A few crumbs under the toaster or a sticky spot on the counter is enough to keep them coming back. Wipe counters after food preparation, sweep or vacuum floors regularly, and avoid leaving pet food out overnight.

## Store Food in Sealed Containers

Open bags of sugar, cereal, or pet food are invitations. Transfer dry goods into airtight containers. Even items in original packaging can be more secure inside a sealed bin.

## Address Moisture

Many ant species are drawn to moisture as much as food. Fix leaky faucets, wipe up standing water around the sink, and check under the refrigerator and dishwasher for damp spots.

## Seal Entry Points

Ants can enter through tiny gaps. Inspect around windows, doors, plumbing penetrations, and baseboards. Caulk or weatherstripping can close many of the easy routes into the kitchen.

## Be Careful with DIY Sprays

Over-the-counter sprays can kill visible ants but often scatter the rest of the colony and do little for the nest. Bait stations, when used correctly, can be more effective because worker ants carry the bait back to the colony. Even so, persistent problems usually benefit from a professional assessment.

## When to Call for Help

If you are seeing ants daily despite good sanitation, or if you notice carpenter ants (larger ants that can damage wood), it is worth having the situation evaluated. [Ant control service](/services/ant-control) can identify the species and target the colony rather than just the foragers you see on the counter.

A clean, dry, well-sealed kitchen goes a long way. Combine those habits with targeted treatment when needed, and most homes can stay comfortably ant-free.
