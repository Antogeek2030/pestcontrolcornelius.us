Termites are a quiet threat in many parts of North Carolina. Subterranean termites, the most common type in this region, often go unnoticed until damage becomes visible. Catching activity early can make a significant difference in the scope and cost of treatment.

## Mud Tubes

One of the clearest signs of subterranean termites is the presence of mud tubes. These pencil-sized tunnels of soil and saliva are built by termites to travel between their underground colony and a wood food source while staying protected from open air. Look for them along foundation walls, in crawl spaces, and near wood that contacts the ground.

## Discarded Wings

After a swarm, reproductive termites shed their wings. Finding piles of small, translucent wings near windowsills, doorways, or light sources in the spring can indicate a nearby colony that has produced swarmers.

## Hollow or Damaged Wood

Termites eat wood from the inside out. Tapping on baseboards, door frames, or structural wood that sounds hollow or papery can be a warning sign. In advanced cases you may see blistered or darkened wood surfaces.

## Frass and Droppings

Drywood termites (less common in this area but still present) leave behind small, pellet-like droppings called frass. Subterranean termites typically do not leave visible frass in the same way, but any unusual wood debris should be investigated.

## What to Do If You Suspect Termites

Avoid disturbing potential mud tubes or damaged areas before an inspection—this can make it harder to assess the extent of activity. A professional inspection can confirm whether termites are present and recommend appropriate next steps.
